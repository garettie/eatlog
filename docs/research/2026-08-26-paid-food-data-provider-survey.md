# Paid food-data provider survey

Research date: 2026-08-26

This note answers [Survey viable paid food-data providers](https://github.com/garettie/eatlog/issues/6). It uses provider-owned pricing pages, API references, terms, and catalog pages. Search-result snippets and third-party API directories were not used as evidence.

## Question and hard gates

Eatlog needs one remote catalog with text search and barcode lookup, branded and generic foods, practical portions, calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat. Restaurant data helps but is not mandatory. The catalog must work for an international consumer app and show real Philippine evidence. Eatlog will normally keep the provider credential in a Cloudflare Worker. A user may permanently keep and export the name, portion, calories, and big-three macros of a confirmed log.

The production offer must stay at or below US$25 per month at expected low hobby traffic. It cannot require an annual or enterprise minimum, a provider-managed end-user account, more than one paid production catalog, or terms that prevent consumer display, Worker proxying, necessary short caching, or permanent confirmed nutrition snapshots.

Evidence labels in this note mean:

- **Verified:** a current first-party page or API specification states or demonstrates the fact.
- **Provider claim:** the provider states a scale, quality, or coverage claim that this survey did not independently test.
- **Sales-gated:** the provider publishes no answer and requires a quote, written exception, or enabled account.
- **Not publicly verified:** the reviewed first-party material does not answer the question. This is not evidence that the capability is absent.
- **Technical inference:** the documented HTTPS authentication flow can run from a Worker. Contract permission still needs to be checked separately.

## Answer

No public production offer clears every hard gate.

Four providers merit conditional follow-up. This is a due-diligence order, not the final provider ranking.

1. **Chomp** has the best public fit for Eatlog's retained history. Its terms expressly allow a user-selected food name, calories, and macros to remain in personal history, including after termination. Its own catalog exposes a Philippines country filter and Philippine products with EANs, serving sizes, and macros. The blocker is economics. Commercial name and ingredient search starts on Standard at US$25 per month plus US$0.001 per request, so any production use exceeds the cap. Limited is usage-priced but barcode-only and non-commercial without express authorization. Chomp remains viable only if it grants a commercial custom or Limited exception with search and an all-in bill no higher than US$25. See [Qualify Chomp's commercial terms for Eatlog](https://github.com/garettie/eatlog/issues/13). ([pricing](https://chompthis.com/api/), [terms](https://chompthis.com/api/terms.php), [Philippines catalog](https://chompthis.com/search/country.php?q=Philippines))
2. **FatSecret** has the strongest documented functional and geographic fit. Premier includes regional datasets, barcode lookup, branded and generic foods, restaurant foods, multiple servings, and a PH region. The blockers are sales-gated price and incompatible default retention terms. Public Premier pricing is "upon request," and the standard terms require all content except a short list of identifiers to be replaced or deleted within 24 hours and deleted when the license ends. FatSecret remains viable only with a quote under the cap and a written permanent confirmed-snapshot exception. See [Qualify FatSecret's Philippines offer for Eatlog](https://github.com/garettie/eatlog/issues/14). ([editions](https://platform.fatsecret.com/api-editions), [PH region](https://platform.fatsecret.com/docs/guides/localization), [terms](https://platform.fatsecret.com/terms), [storable fields](https://platform.fatsecret.com/docs/guides/storable-data))
3. **Edamam** is the only public full-search plan below the cap. Food Database Enterprise Basic is US$14 month to month and includes 100,000 calls per month, text and UPC/EAN search, packaged, generic, fast-food, and restaurant data. Basic may cache only food ID and label. The US$69 Core plan adds calories and macro caching, but cached data may be used only in an end user's password-protected account, only while an eligible subscription stays active. Edamam's FAQ requires customers to stop using all supplied nutrition after cancellation. Edamam publishes no Philippine market or catalog evidence. It remains viable only if Edamam extends snapshot rights to Basic without the password, active-subscription, or post-cancellation restrictions, and a trial passes Philippine cases. See [Qualify Edamam's Basic plan for Eatlog](https://github.com/garettie/eatlog/issues/15). ([plan and caching table](https://developer.edamam.com/food-database-api), [FAQ](https://developer.edamam.com/api/faq), [OpenAPI specification](https://api.edamam.com/doc/open-api/food-db-v2.yaml))
4. **Nutritics** documents text search, UPC-A lookup, brand, portions, macros, and menu data in its API explorer, but public price, minimum commitment, rate limits, retention rights, and Philippine coverage could not be verified. It remains viable only if a direct commercial offer clears every gate. See [Qualify Nutritics' API offer for Eatlog](https://github.com/garettie/eatlog/issues/17). ([API explorer](https://www.nutritics.com/api/v1.2/explorer/))

[Compare Eatlog's qualified provider trials](https://github.com/garettie/eatlog/issues/16) should run only offers that first clear the commercial, architecture, retention, and cost gates.

## International and Philippine evidence

- **Chomp, verified catalog evidence:** Chomp's advanced search offers a Philippines country filter. Its public Philippines result page contains several products and EANs. The Lucky Me Instant Pancit Canton Chili-mansi record is tagged Philippines and contains barcode `4807770273698`, an 80 g serving, energy, protein, carbohydrates, and fat. This proves some Philippine data, not useful coverage. ([country filter](https://chompthis.com/api/build.php), [Philippines results](https://chompthis.com/search/country.php?q=Philippines), [Lucky Me record](https://chompthis.com/product/?id=588842))
- **FatSecret, verified market entitlement shape:** the localization reference lists `PH` as Philippines with English Regional as its default language. Localization is a premium feature enabled for selected accounts and results can be filtered by region. The Premier page says every country dataset contains generic, composite generic, branded, and restaurant foods. This proves a named Philippine regional dataset exists, not its hit rate. ([localization](https://platform.fatsecret.com/docs/guides/localization), [catalog FAQ](https://platform.fatsecret.com/platform-api))
- **Calorie API, limited catalog evidence:** the provider's official public search returns five records for `Philippines`, including an unverified Del Monte Philippines branded record with barcode `4800024570741`, a 100 g serving, portions, calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat. Every returned record has `is_verified: false`. This proves some Philippine records, not coverage or quality. ([public search](https://calorieapiadmin.com/api/v1/public/search/foods?q=Philippines))
- **Edamam, unavailable evidence:** the current product page and OpenAPI schema expose no country or region parameter and name no Philippines dataset. The global size statements on the product page do not prove Philippine coverage. ([product page](https://developer.edamam.com/food-database-api), [OpenAPI](https://api.edamam.com/doc/open-api/food-db-v2.yaml))
- **Nutritionix, evidence against the target:** the instant-search `branded_region` parameter supports only `1=US` and `2=UK`; no region filter defaults to US. Nutritionix markets curated common international foods, but its Global Locales sheet contains no Philippines, Filipino, or Tagalog entry. ([instant endpoint](https://docx.syndigo.com/developers/docs/instant-endpoint), [Global Locales](https://pages.syndigo.com/rs/539-CDH-942/images/Nutritionix%20Global%20Locales.pdf?version=0))
- **MyNetDiary, evidence against the target:** its licensable catalog names only the United States, Canada, United Kingdom, and Australia. ([licensing](https://www.mynetdiary.com/food-database.html))
- **Nutritics, Spoonacular, LogMeal, Passio, API Ninjas, and Trustwell:** reviewed first-party pages contain no Philippines market, filter, named data source, product example, or measured Philippine result. Words such as "global," "international," and "localized" were not counted as Philippine evidence.

## Screened-out non-paid sources

USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts are useful public data sources, not candidates for the requested paid production catalog. FoodData Central offers a free API and publishes a default limit of 1,000 requests/hour per IP. Its Global Branded Food Products Database currently names the United States and New Zealand, not the Philippines. Open Food Facts provides an open API under its database terms and nonprofit model. Neither resolves this ticket's paid-provider selection, and the map already requires the chosen paid provider to replace them in normal remote results. ([FoodData Central API guide](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/), [Global Branded Food documentation](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/GBFPD_Documentation/), [Open Food Facts API conditions](https://support.openfoodfacts.org/help/en-gb/12-donnees-api/94-y-a-t-il-des-conditions-pour-utiliser-l-api), [Open Food Facts economic model](https://support.openfoodfacts.org/help/en-gb/9-open-food-facts/26-what-is-open-food-facts-economic-model))
